# What Schedule VII Really Means for Corporate CSR Spend

If your company has recently crossed into CSR applicability under the Companies Act, 2013,
the first question is usually the simplest one: what are we actually allowed to spend this
money on? The answer lives in **Schedule VII** — and it's broader, and more specific, than
most first-time CSR teams expect.

## Who has to comply

Section 135 applies to companies that, in the preceding financial year, meet any one of
three thresholds:

- Net worth of ₹500 crore or more, or
- Turnover of ₹1,000 crore or more, or
- Net profit of ₹5 crore or more

Once a company crosses any of these, it must constitute a CSR Committee, adopt a CSR policy,
and spend at least **2% of average net profit** from the preceding three financial years on
CSR activities.

## What counts as an eligible activity

Schedule VII lists broad categories, not a rigid activity list — which is both a strength
and a source of confusion. Eligible areas include:

- Eradicating hunger, poverty, and malnutrition; promoting healthcare and sanitation
- Promoting education, including special education and employment-enhancing vocational skills
- Promoting gender equality and empowering women
- Ensuring environmental sustainability and ecological balance
- Protecting national heritage, art, and culture
- Rural development projects
- Disaster management, including relief, rehabilitation, and reconstruction

Activities have to fit within these categories — and critically, they generally can't be
activities the company would have undertaken anyway in the normal course of business.

## The part teams get wrong

The most common compliance gap isn't picking an ineligible cause — it's **documentation**.
Schedule VII spend has to be traceable: which project, which implementing partner, how much,
and what impact resulted. That's the gap NITICSR's Verification Vault and matching workflow
are built to close, so reporting is a byproduct of doing the work rather than a scramble at
year-end.

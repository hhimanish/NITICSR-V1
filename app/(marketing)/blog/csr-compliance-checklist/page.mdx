# A CSR Compliance Checklist for Indian Companies

CSR compliance in India isn't a single filing — it's a cycle of decisions, spend, and
disclosure that repeats every financial year. Here's the shape of that cycle.

## 1. Confirm applicability and budget

At the start of the year, confirm whether your company still meets a Section 135 threshold,
and calculate the required 2% spend based on average net profit of the preceding three years.

## 2. Constitute and update your CSR Committee

Ensure your CSR Committee is properly constituted (or confirm the board-level exemption
applies, for smaller qualifying companies) and that your CSR policy is current.

## 3. Register implementing partners

NGOs and implementing agencies generally need to be registered on the MCA portal (Form
CSR-1) before a company can route CSR funds to them. Confirm this status before committing
budget — not after.

## 4. Track spend against ongoing vs. one-time projects

Unspent CSR amounts are treated differently depending on whether they relate to an ongoing
project. Ongoing-project unspent amounts typically move to a designated Unspent CSR
Account; other unspent amounts may need to be transferred to a specified Schedule VII fund.
Both paths carry deadlines — track them from the day funds go unspent, not at year-end.

## 5. Document impact, not just disbursement

A wire transfer isn't evidence of CSR activity. Keep records of what the funds achieved:
beneficiaries reached, milestones hit, and any third-party impact assessment where
applicable.

## 6. File your CSR-2 disclosure

Form CSR-2 is filed as an addendum to your annual financial statement filing, capturing CSR
spend details for the year. This is typically where poor documentation during the year
becomes painfully visible — plan for it in advance, not in the week it's due.

## 7. Review and carry lessons forward

Before the next cycle starts, review which partnerships worked, which caused reporting
friction, and adjust your NGO due diligence process accordingly.

*This checklist is a general educational overview, not a substitute for advice from your
company secretary, auditor, or CSR compliance counsel — confirm current forms, deadlines,
and thresholds before relying on them.*
